### Notes from the 2026-08 batch

- **Casino Royale hit "masterpiece" off the watchlist** — the calibration works.
- **True-story-jeopardy shelf validated hard**: Saving Private Ryan (masterpiece) +
  Chernobyl (great). Chernobyl also refines "slow = boring": slow is fine when every
  scene advances a ticking disaster — it's idle *atmosphere* that bores, not pace.
- **Sopranos great / Godfather no**: same subject, opposite temperature. Tony is a
  rooting-interest antihero with wants and jokes, every episode has an engine;
  Godfather is mythic ritual observed from a distance. Warm momentum beats cold myth.
- **Forrest Gump great**: episodic structure forgiven because it's the purest
  underdog-empowerment arc in cinema (v4 flagship) + concrete want (Jenny).
- **Django ("what a fucking movie") vs Inglourious (merely good)**: one burning
  single-man want (rescue Broomhilda) vs diffuse ensemble talk-suspense. Perfectly
  consistent with v3/v4 — Tarantino works here when he's a ride with a want-engine.
- **WALL-E great**: want + underdog + sincere, wordless for 40 minutes and still an
  engine.
- **The Burial great** (added 2026-08-04): courtroom + underdog-team-vs-corporation +
  comedy in one film, and a true story — triple-validation. Also the first "funny
  courtroom" data point: laughs work best for you when they ride a real jeopardy
  engine (consistent with the comedy profile). Unlocks a legal-underdog shelf.

## The theory

**v1:** momentum + sincerity → killed by Truman Show.
**v2:** concrete want → killed by Whiplash.
**v3 + v3.1 (still core):** likeable person, external jeopardy, plays as a ride;
seam-sensitive (plot can't show its hands); threat stays serious even when the film is
funny; true-story jeopardy beats "made up" plots.

### v4 hypothesis — the "greatest"-tier separator: UNDERDOG EMPOWERMENT

What distinguishes the wow tier from the merely-good tier: in every top-tier film, a
likeable ordinary person / underdog **turns out to be capable of extraordinary
things**:

- Neo: office drone → the One (the purest version; probably why it's the GOAT)
- Tony Stark: dying in a cave → builds the suit from scraps
- Marty McFly: teenager outwits time itself
- Frank Abagnale: teenage kid outruns the FBI
- Kale (Disturbia): grounded teen outwits a serial killer
- Cooper: washed-up pilot becomes the ghost/savior across spacetime
- Juror 8: one ordinary man flips eleven
- Even Toy Story: a displaced toy wins his place back

The "good"-tier films are jeopardy rides *without* empowerment — pure survival (Alien,
Jaws, A Quiet Place, 10 Cloverfield Lane). Well-executed suspense caps at "good";
suspense + wish-fulfillment/competence-fantasy is what produces "greatest."

Corollary (The Matrix vs TDK): ideas/philosophy are fine — even great — when they're
**fuel for the ride** (simulation theory exists so Neo can bend it) rather than the
ride's replacement (TDK delivers its ideas as speeches). The Matrix is a thesis film
that never lectures; that's why it works here and TDK doesn't.

### Comedy profile (new dimension)

Data: Borat (funniest ever), Ace Ventura, Happy Gilmore, Home Alone, Silicon Valley
(best show), Toy Story.

- **Silly over dry.** Total-commitment physical performers (Carrey, Sandler, Cohen) and
  gag density. Not deadpan-ironic, not rom-com, not Wes Anderson.
- **The engine shows up even in comedy.** Every comedy favorite has a want/jeopardy
  spine: Happy wants to save grandma's house; Kevin defends the home from burglars
  (Disturbia-as-comedy!); Ace is on a case; Borat is on a quest (Pamela); Silicon
  Valley's company nearly dies every episode — it's a comedy structured like a
  thriller, underdogs vs Hooli. The taste is consistent across genres: underdog +
  scheme/jeopardy + escalation, plus laughs.
- Metric is probably laugh count, not cleverness — but SV shows smart is welcome when
  it's also gag-dense and momentum-driven.

## Anti-recommendations

- 2001, Blade Runner, most Kubrick, late Malick, slow cinema — idle atmosphere.
- A.I. — Spielberg's parable, his Truman Show.
- Symbol-protagonist / thesis-as-lecture films, warm or cold.
- Dry/ironic/deadpan comedy (Wes Anderson shelf) — predicted miss per comedy profile.

Not "don't get them" — the filter is calibrated, not deficient. Taste isn't a test you
pass; it's a filter you learn.
